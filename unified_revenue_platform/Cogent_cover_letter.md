# Cover Letter

**To:** The Editors, *Cogent Business & Management*

**Re:** Transferred submission of "The Unified Revenue Platform: A System-Agnostic Reference Architecture for Integrated Revenue Decision Support in B2B Enterprises"

---

Dear Editors,

I am pleased to submit the above manuscript for consideration as a Research Article in *Cogent Business & Management*, following a transfer through the Taylor & Francis Article Transfer Service from the *Journal of Decision Systems*, where the editors judged the work meritorious but outside that journal's scope.

**Contribution and fit.** The paper is a design-science contribution to business and management information systems. It proposes the Unified Revenue Platform, a system-agnostic four-layer reference architecture — ingestion, dimension engineering, intelligence, and presentation — that integrates the full revenue decision-support stack of a B2B enterprise (CRM ingestion, fiscal and pipeline dimension engineering, hierarchical target allocation, behavioral commit reconciliation, and machine-learning forecasting) into a single coherent, vendor-reversible system. The paper derives explicit design requirements, states three governing design principles (single source of truth, layered transformation, and compensation-plan alignment), specifies each layer's responsibilities and interfaces, and evaluates an open-source reference implementation against those requirements using a calibrated simulation and an end-to-end scalability characterization to 10,000 organizational nodes. The topic — how enterprises structure analytics, forecasting, and quota processes so that sales organizations can act on them — sits squarely within the journal's operations and management-information-systems scope, and the practical orientation of the work suits the journal's applied readership.

**Relationship to my other work (full transparency).** Two of the platform's intelligence modules — a directed-acyclic-graph method for hierarchical target allocation and a manager-level commit-bias reconciliation method — are formalized and evaluated in a separate companion manuscript currently under review at the *Journal of Revenue and Pricing Management* (Karwa, 2026; SSRN 6626318). The present manuscript does not re-derive or re-claim those methods or their component-level results; it treats them as interchangeable, cited components and contributes the integrating architecture, design principles, forecast-submission workflow, and reproducible reference implementation. The two papers are complementary and non-overlapping in their claims.

**Evaluation note.** The evaluation is an explicitly simulation-based design-science demonstration using a synthetic data generator calibrated to parameters reported in the literature; no real customer data are used, and the paper makes no empirical claims about a specific deployment. The complete implementation, including the data generator and reproduction scripts, is openly available, and a Data Availability Statement is included in the manuscript.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration elsewhere. Generative AI use is disclosed in the manuscript in accordance with the Taylor & Francis AI Policy. The author declares no competing interests, received no external funding, and is aware of the journal's open-access model and associated Article Publishing Charge.
